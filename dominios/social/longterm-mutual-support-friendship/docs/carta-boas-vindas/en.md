You've arrived.

Arrived at a project that began in an empty folder, on an August Saturday in 2026, with a sentence about a bridge. Today the bridge has a constitution, specifications, products, an operating system that doesn't exist yet and a phone that hasn't been born — but all of them are written, dated and open.

This document is the invitation for you to come in.

## What this place is

A **personal** initiative by Cleiton Moura Loura, a Brazilian citizen, prototype architect. No party, no company, no government, no flag. An ordinary citizen who decided to build instead of complain, and chose to do it in three languages — Portuguese, English and Chinese — because the bridge he wants to cross has two sides.

## What already exists

**An ecosystem of 8 public repositories** with ~200 specifications, 33 documentary bases, an engineering constitution and a development pipeline. All AGPL/CC BY-SA, all under the authorship of whoever built it, all trilingual.

**A specified device**: the Teia Phone — open chassis built for a decade, modules that rotate, one port, a canonical Linux system, the entire games library, local AI, and a chain of APUs waiting for Chinese RISC-V silicon to mature.

**An energy foundation**: from the definition of joule to Dyson scale, from civilization as client to the half-watt smartphone — with the avatar as energy's public servant.

**Two derivative products**: a mesh node powered by light (the offline web's infrastructure) and a 200 kWh/year modular refrigerator (conservation personified).

## How the house works

**Spec Driven Development** — no line of code without a specification governing it. No specification without a verification path. You see what will be built before building it.

**Trilingual by conception** — every document is born in three languages in the same commit. If you read Chinese, you are not a translation; you are an origin.

**Contribution-first** — whatever you need to exist, goes upstream to the house. Fork is last resort, with burden of proof.

**The sieve** — your suggestion enters through a technical filter of 7 sieves. If it passes, it becomes spec. If it doesn't, you get a response with reason. The graveyard is documented.

**Rust** — the official language. If you program Rust, you're home.

## What we need

**Translators** — always. Chinese speakers who read Portuguese, Brazilians who read Chinese, anyone who bridges two worlds. Translation is a first-class contribution.

**Rust programmers** — the avatar (the energy agent) is specified and waiting to be born. The mesh node needs firmware. TeiaOS needs someone who loves mainline.

**Hardware engineers** — the MOD specs exist; the schematics don't. If you design boards, the house has 19 requirements waiting.

**People** — to review specs, discuss decisions, point out errors, disagree with arguments. The sieve exists for this: your voice enters, is filtered with fairness, and becomes part of the work.

## What you get

Not money — the foundation exists but has no funds yet. You get:

- **Eternal credit**: your name in Git history, in the changelog, in the work. The AGPL license guarantees your work is never appropriated without your name.
- **A community that respects authorship**: the house was born from the pain of seeing ideas captured, and the entire constitution exists so this never happens to anyone here.
- **A project with meaning**: not another app; an honest attempt to build technology that serves people instead of capturing them.

## How to enter

1. **Read the constitution** — `docs/spec-driven-development.md` (or `.en.md` or `.zh.md`)
2. **Choose a repo** — start with what you know
3. **Open an issue** — introduce yourself, say what you can do, what you want to learn
4. **Translate something** — the cheapest and most valuable contribution
5. **Or just observe** — the house is open; entering is optional

## The sentence that sums it up

One day someone will pick up a Teia Phone, snap it into a dock, and work on a real Linux terminal. They'll play the entire Steam library, run a local language model, and share internet through the mesh with a neighbor. And when that happens, the person won't know it started with an empty folder, a Saturday, and a sentence about a bridge.

But you know. Because you were here.
