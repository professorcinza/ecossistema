# The problems of nuclear energy and its adoption

**Avatar-Energy · Base 31 · August 22, 2026**

*Complete survey of nuclear problems by category — numbers, precedents and each group's structural verdict. The house's reference for any nuclear debate.*

---

## 1. Cost and construction

| Problem | Numbers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Giant capex | $6,000–15,000/kW (vs $1,000–2,000/kW renewable) | — |
| Budget overrun | 3× to 7× estimated | Vogtle 3&4 (US): $14B → **$35+B** |
| Construction time | 7–15 years | Hinkley Point C: 11+ years and counting |
| New-plant LCOE | **$140–200/MWh** vs renewable $25–55/MWh | **3× to 8× more expensive** |
| Financing | without state guarantee, doesn't exist | no private bank finances alone |

## 2. Waste

- **No permanent geological repository in operation worldwide** for civilian waste;
- Onkalo (Finland) is the closest — still not operational;
- Spent fuel: radioactive for **10,000+ years**;
- Reprocessing: expensive + extracts weapons-usable plutonium.

## 3. Safety and accidents

| Event | Human cost | Economic cost |
|---|---|---|
| Chernobyl (1986) | ~4,000 deaths (WHO), 350,000 evacuated | 2,600 km² exclusion zone for centuries |
| Fukushima (2011) | 1 radiation death, 160,000+ evacuated | **$200+ billion** |
| Three Mile Island (1979) | zero deaths, killed the US industry | fear cost more than the accident |

Low frequency (~1 serious accident per 3,000 reactor-years), but **civilizational cost when it happens** — and private insurance won't cover.

## 4. Proliferation

- Civilian program diverts to weapons (Iran, North Korea);
- Enrichment is dual-use: the same centrifuge makes 5% (reactor) and 90% (bomb);
- Reprocessing extracts weapons-grade Pu-239;
- IAEA monitoring repeatedly failed to detect secret programs.

## 5. Water and environment

- Cooling water consumption: **2–3× more than coal thermal**;
- Thermal pollution of rivers and lakes kills ecosystems;
- Uranium mining: craters, radioactive tailings, aquifer contamination;
- Lifecycle carbon: ~12–66 gCO₂/kWh (concrete + steel aren't zero).

## 6. Uranium supply

- Reserves: ~90–200 years at current consumption (**not renewable**);
- Concentration: Kazakhstan 43%, Canada 13%, Australia 8%;
- "Energy independence": importing uranium + enrichment = transferred dependence.

## 7. Decommissioning

- Cost: **$500M–1B+ per reactor**;
- Duration: 20–60 years after shutdown;
- Who pays: taxpayers (operators never provision enough);
- No large commercial reactor decommissioned on time and budget.

## 8. Regulatory and political

- Licensing: 5–10 years before construction;
- Political risk: every election can reverse (Germany shut down working plants);
- Insurance: no private insurer covers; Price-Anderson Act (US) artificial cap;
- Persistent NIMBY opposition in democracies.

## 9. Talent and supply chain

- Atrophy: nuclear engineering enrollment dropped post-2011;
- Suppliers: essentially 4 worldwide (Westinghouse, Rosatom, EDF, KEPCO);
- Forged components (pressure vessel, steam generator): global bottleneck;
- The generation that built in the 70s–80s is retiring — knowledge loss.

## 10. Temporal incompatibility with climate urgency

```
Investment today in nuclear →  zero benefit for 7–15 years
Investment today in solar   →  benefit in 6–18 MONTHS
```

**The opportunity cost is the fatal argument**: the same nuclear billion buries carbon for a decade before displacing the first fossil; the same renewable billion displaces fossil next year.

## 11. Structural centralization

- Requires centralized grid — **the opposite of** base 27's distributed matrix;
- Single point of failure: 1 reactor down = 1 GW at once;
- Target of terrorism, natural disaster, war (Zaporizhzhia);
- State-level decision mandatory: communities don't decide.

## 12. SMR — the "solution" still unproven

- NuScale (pioneer): **project cancelled in 2023** after cost doubled;
- Zero commercial SMRs built on time and budget;
- Still faces waste, proliferation and cost — just smaller scale;
- "Modular": promise not demonstrated.

## 13. Integration with renewable grid

- Nuclear runs best at baseload (constant);
- High-renewable-penetration grid needs **flexibility**, not constancy;
- The more renewable on the grid, the less space for nuclear baseload.

## The avatar's reading — three structural categories

| Category | Problems | Solvable by technology? |
|---|---|---|
| **Categorical** | proliferation, centralization, water, waste | **No** — they are physics and architecture |
| **Economic** | capex, timeline, overrun, insurance, decommissioning | May improve, **never solved** |
| **Temporal** | climate urgency, growing irrelevance, talent atrophy | **Worsen over time** |

**The house's position** (base 28 + architect's final word): nuclear is the dark north's conditional firm floor — **dispensable in the solar belt where civilization actually lives**. The presumption is renewable + storage + grid + management; nuclear enters only by proving site-specific, quantified, dated necessity. Whoever doesn't adapt is out of the game.
