# Open Collaboration Charter

**From a prototype architect to all who wish to build with him**

*Languages:* [English](en.md) · [Português](pt.md) · [中文](zh.md)

---

## Preamble

The author of this charter is a prototype architect — someone who conceives and designs the first shapes of ideas so that other hands may make them real. This charter complements this project's Unilateral Declaration of Commitment and sets out the terms under which he offers his creative capacity to the world: in collaboration with people, never at the disposal of anyone.

## Article 1 — What is offered

The author places the following capacities at the service of the project — and of anyone who wishes to collaborate:

1. **Creation of prototypes** of diverse kinds: software, hardware, documents, systems, and processes;
2. **Co-creation** with people and teams who wish to develop those prototypes into reality;
3. **Documentation and sharing** of the knowledge produced, publicly and openly.

## Article 2 — How collaboration works

All collaboration shall follow these terms:

1. **With people, not abstractions.** One collaborates with identifiable people, groups, and institutions acting in their own name — not with "nations", "governments", or faceless entities;
2. **Common agreement.** Every collaboration arises from the free and informed consent of all parties;
3. **Mutual benefit.** Every collaboration must create fair and balanced value for everyone involved;
4. **Documented authorship.** All work is publicly versioned (Git history), with authorship, dates, and a verifiable record of priority;
5. **Explicit licenses.** No work is published without a clear license, chosen case by case.

## Article 3 — What is not offered

Lest any doubt remain, the author declares that:

1. **His intellect is not "at the disposal"** of any nation, company, or institution — one collaborates with him; one does not capture him;
2. **No license transfers authorship.** Licenses grant the *use* of a work, never *ownership* of its authorship;
3. **No collaborator obtains exclusivity** over the author or his work — what is published here remains open to all, on the same terms.

## Article 4 — Intellectual property

1. **Software** shall by default be published under free-software licenses that require authorship credit (such as GPL or AGPL, as appropriate);
2. **Documents and other content** are published under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0);
3. The repository's public version history constitutes a permanent, timestamped record of the authorship and priority of the ideas published in it.

## Article 5 — The invitation

People, groups, companies, and institutions — Brazilian, Chinese, or of any nationality — who wish to co-create prototypes on these terms are invited to come forward through the project repository.
