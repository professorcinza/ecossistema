# Unilateral Declaration of Commitment

**Brazil–China Friendship and Mutual Support**

*Languages:* [English](en.md) · [Português](pt.md) · [中文](zh.md)

---

## Preamble

The undersigned makes this declaration fully aware of his condition: an ordinary person, a Brazilian citizen, with no mandate, no public office, and no power to negotiate or decide on behalf of Brazil, China, or anyone else. This is not a negotiating table, and nothing expressed here imposes any duty, obligation, or expectation upon any third party.

This is a **unilateral commitment**: a set of commitments freely assumed by a single person, in his own name, in pursuit of a future situation of bilateral cooperation between Brazilians and Chinese, founded on mutual support. A commitment of this nature only becomes bilateral on the day someone, of their own free will, chooses to reciprocate it.

## Article 1 — Purpose

To foster, by peaceful, transparent, and voluntary means, the conditions of trust and respect necessary for bilateral cooperation between Brazilians and Chinese — based on common agreement and mutual benefit — to one day take shape.

## Article 2 — Commitments of the declarant

I commit, unilaterally and in my own name, to:

1. always act with **honesty and transparency**, publicly documenting whatever I do in this repository;
2. **never claim to represent** Brazil, China, or any public or private institution;
3. **respect** Brazilian law, Chinese law, and applicable international treaties;
4. treat the cultures, languages, and traditions of both countries with **curiosity, humility, and respect**;
5. only put forward proposals that create **fair and balanced value** for all parties involved;
6. welcome any form of reciprocation of this commitment with gratitude — **never demanding or presuming it**.

## Article 3 — Intended fields of action

The declarant initially intends to act in the following fields:

- **Cultural exchange** — languages, arts, cuisine, traditions;
- **Academic exchange** — study, research, and science communication;
- **Commercial exchange** — connections between small entrepreneurs and producers;
- **Technology and innovation** — free software and open knowledge projects;
- **Sustainability** — environmental initiatives of common interest.

## Article 4 — Absence of power and representation

This declaration issues from no authority and confers no power. It creates no rights, obligations, affiliations, or representations before the Government of Brazil, the Government of the People's Republic of China, or any public or private institution. Its reach is limited to the conscience and will of the person who signs it.

## Article 5 — An open invitation

This declaration ends not with a demand but with an invitation: anyone — Brazilian, Chinese, or of any other nationality — who, of their own free will and in their own name, wishes to reciprocate these commitments is welcome to join the initiative through the project repository.
